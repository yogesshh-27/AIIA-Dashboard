Intervention  Skin wash using Soap and warm water, Phanta Soaking, Pre IMLD yoga exercises, Indian manual lymph drainage, Compression bandaging and Post IMLD yoga exercises  Dermatology counseling: Patients attended a counseling session before deciding on treatment. During this process, patients and their family members got complete details of the treatment program. Soap and water wash: Meticulous soap and water wash every day was given by a multipurpose health worker, assisted by the patientâ€™s attendant Phanta soaking: Following the limb wash, the affected limb is immersed in an ayurvedic skin care â€˜â€˜phantaâ€™â€™ solution for 20 minutes. Care of Bacterial entry point: In cases of fungal intertrigo apply clotrimazole 1% cream or amorolfine hydrochloride in sever intractable cases. Ayurvedic herbal dusting powders (avachoornana) are applied on toe web spaces and in the gaps of large folds to absorb moisture. Rasnadi choorna or panchavalkala choorna can be used for absorbing moisture in interdigital web spaces and creases of folds. Rasnadi Choorna is a coarsely powdered mixture of 19 herbs, the main ingredients of which are Pluchea lanceolata root (rasna) and Cedrus deodara stem (devadaru). Many patients have associated gravitational eczema. They were treated with topical betamethasone dipropionate 0.05%. Oozing eczema is initially treated with topical steroids applied under occlusion. Ulcers were treated with cefadroxil 500mg bid x 5 days. Unhealthy granulation tissues and slough on the base of the ulcer are treated with jathyadi thaila, an oil based preparation made of 19 herbs. Yoga exercises: Lymph drainage is achieved by a combination of yoga exercises. Eight yoga exercises (asanas) and five special breathing techniques (pranayamas) are practiced before IMLD, and 10 asanas and five pranayamas are performed afterwards. Yoga DVDs and narrative hand outs are available in IAD but yoga was coached by a therapist during the treatment. Indian manual lymph drainage (IMLD): This is a skin care procedure administered in ayurveda, classified under the broad category of abhyanga . Abhyanga involves massage of the affected parts of the body using both palms while the patient lies supine on a massage table. Part 1 of IMLD (called unmardhana in ayurveda) is a type of non-oil massage that requires the masseur to apply a â€˜â€˜squeezingâ€™â€™pressure using both palms and fingers and moving from the tip of the toes to the upper edge of the lymphoedema. This non-oil massage is performed for 10 minutes with the patient in a supine position. Part 2 of IMLD (udhwarthana ) isa massage using oil. In udhwarthana, oil is smeared to the skin, and the limb is massaged in the opposite direction to hair with more pressure than in general abhyanga. Compression bandaging: After IMLD, disinfected cotton cloth is wrapped around the limb in order to prevent the direct contact with oil of the compression bandages. Brief Summary  

The project will end by 30th April 2012.  Now the study is in the last phase of patient follow up. Write up below is extracts from the report of the evaluation committee approved by the DG of CCRAS, Department of AYUSH

 Introduction

250 districts in over 15 Indian states are endemic to Lymphatic Filariasis (LF) harboring worlds 1/3 of disease burden. Morbidity control program of GPELF (Global program for elimination of Lymphatic filariasis) being in infancy, no major public health intervention exists except surgery to a small number of patients and advise to wash their limbs. CCRAS (Central Council For Research in Ayurvedic Sciences) under department of AYUSH , Government of India funded a community based intervention trial in two most endemic districts having high poverty index, Alleppey in Kerala and Gulbarga in Karnataka . 1000 patients having unilateral or bilateral lower limb lymphoedema having either grade 2 early , grade 2 late or grade 3 were treated using low cost, self care and home based integrated treatment combining skin wash, ayurvedic skincare, yoga therapy and compression bandaging and bacterial entry point treatment of allopathy. The treatment was implemented by IAD (Institute of Applied Dermatology) at community health centers level at Cherthala in Alleppey and Malkhed in Gulbarga as a public private partnership. This pilot project is in the last phase after completion of patientsâ€™ recruitment and treatment; patients are following up treatment.

The project was implemented in two most endemic districts of southern India based on the National Filaria Control Program data: Alleppey of Kerala and Gulbarga of Karnataka. In Gulbarga 3.3% of entire population is affected by this disease. The project is implemented in the model of Innovative Care for Chronic Conditions of WHO. The grading of lymphodema and the conceptual framework of management was as per the guidelines of International Society of Lymphology Consensus Statement 2009. As there are no community level programs for LF in India except an advice to wash their limbs and surgery to minority of patients, this is the pilot program to test if AYUSH treatments integrated with allopathy is effective at the rural communities. The study was conducted as non randomized clinical trial with before and after assessment of outcomes. Because the project used at least three objective parameters as outcome measures, in line with Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions (Version 5) recommendations, non-RCT trial with outcome assessment before and after intervention, is an acceptable design in the current set up, albeit that evaluation committee recognizes RCT as the reference standard methodology.

Survey to identify the patients in the community

The project adopted a novel method of rapid survey techniques to indentify the patients in the community. First secondary data was collected from district medical office. This data was customized to Cherthala and Malkhed regions. With prior permission for authorities allied health staff such as ASHA workers, village health nurses, health inspectors etc were educated about the LF morbidity reduction program through their monthly meetings. Same strategy was used to motivate the members of CBOs and other local volunteers. Using their contacts, individual patients and clusters of patients were identified and invited for LF camps. Each such camp was used to create awareness with local social leaders, officials and politicians.

Treatment delivery and community approach of the project

The treatment delivery team was headed by an ayurvedic doctor; assisted by an allopathic staff nurse and master in social work (MSW) to coordinate the community level programs. The patients were selected during LF camps conducted in the villages. Patients of any age, sex, disease grade and duration were recruited. Patients presenting with Acute Dermato Lymphangio Adinitis (cellulitis or filarial fever, also called ADLA) were excluded from the study. All patients received the treatment in the following sequence: soap and water wash, soak with herbalized phanta solution, sequences of yoga exercises with pranayama, Indian manual lymph drainage, compression bandaging and a set of yoga exercise. The treatment was administered as described in international publications titled, â€˜Integrated management of filarial lymphodema for rural communities. Lymphology 2007; 40: 3-13â€™ and â€œIntegrating modern dermatology and Ayurveda in the treatment of vitiligo and lymphedema in India. International Journal of Dermatology, 2011; 50 (3): 310â€“334.â€

Data Collection:

Measurement of instrument is appropriate given the studyâ€™s variables. The scoring method is clearly defined. Data on treatment outcomes in LF are maintained in a Microsoft Access database designed and developed by IAD. They are maintained as changes over each limb which is an international consensus. This database can instantly retrieve information using SPSS 16 analysis. Clinical outcome evaluations used were: 1.By recording base line and follow up girth measurements or volume by water displacement method 2.By recording baseline and follow up photos 3.By recording the frequency of â€˜filarial fever episodesâ€™ also known as inflammatory episode 4.By recording the Quality of Life through a validated questionnaire in those patients who have completed the follow ups. Inclusion and exclusion criteria are clearly defined in the patient population selected for the trial. The project treated 1000 patients so far. However there was a selection bias. The treatment team insisted on the participation of at least one member of the family in the treatment program. So those elderly patients who didnâ€™t have any family support were denied the opportunity to take the treatment. Also those patients who were unable to afford the travel cost to the centre couldnâ€™t take the treatment. Dependent t test was used for comparison of limb volumes changes. The study is not complete as two months of follow up is still remaining. Therefore complete statistical analysis was not possible during this evaluation. Based on the available data the project treated 528 lymphoedematous limbs (400 patients) in Alleppey and 659 limbs (600 patients) in Gulbarga. 50 patients dropped out from treatment either for personal reason or were unable to continue the treatment as they found it difficult to follow all procedures. In Alleppey 117 limbs had more than 7 liters of volume (large limb) and 411 limbs were small limbs less than 7 liters. The volume in large limbs reduced from 10.3 liters from baseline to 7.1 liters by 3 months of follow up, small limbs reduced from 5.8 liters from baseline to 4.6 liters at the end of 3 months of follow up. In terms of percentage, large limbs reduced by 39% at the end of 3 months and small limbs by 17.6%. In Gulbarga volume reduction values were 41.4% for large limbs and 22.6% in small limbs at the end of 3 months Other outcome measures used in the trial changed as follows: history of inflammatory episodes was present in 89.3% of limbs (442 limbs) at baseline in Alleppy and at the end of 3months of follow up 5.6% of limbs (9limbs) had the history of inflammatory episode. In Gulbarga the values were 89.7% (540 limbs) and 0.9% (2 limbs) respectively. Bacterial entry points were observed in 80.1% of limbs (401 limbs) and 13% (21 limbs) at the end of 3 months of treatment. In Gulbarga it was observed in 50.3% (302 limbs) at admission and at the end 3months of treatment in 29% (64 limbs). On reversal of lymphoedema grading following treatment , in Alleppy it was observed that 49.8% (73 limbs) were positive for stemmers sign became 5.6%(9 limbs) at the end of 3 months, the values were 12.8%(84 limbs) and to 2.3%(5 limbs) at the end of 3 months for Gulbarga. Quality of life of patients was assessed using a disease specific questionnaire. This was validated, pretested in IAD adopted from WHODAS and VCRC tools but customized for Indian patients. Data of patients who completed three months of followup where analyzed on seven domains.

Patients in Alleppey generally presented with the oedema up to groin. In Gulbarga 133 limbs had oedema up to groin or mid thigh and 526 limb had oedema limited to below the knee level.

The setting of the study:

Although facilities were very much resource poor and restricted, the team provided treatment to an average of 22 patients per day in Cherthala and 28 per day in Malkhed. The team members initially provided this treatment for 9 days and remaining 5 days were used for training these patients for self care along with their family members. This training is itself the treatment for their morbidity reduction. Therefore the study is replicable in any Indian rural settings.

Conclusion

The treatment is cost effective, easily administrable at remote village level. We estimated a compliance rate of over 40% in the study. However the patients are still following up the treatment and follow up numbers are likely to increase before the project period ends. The reasons for heavy dropout rates are: the distance of patientsâ€™ clusters from the treatment facility although centre was established at the community health centre of the state Government, making access difficult for most patients. Inability to pay for the transport cost, poor connectivity by road are other common reasons. However treatment is effective in the village level as all patients whom evaluators interacted were happy of the outcome and have improved quality of life parameters. This is a model program of self care for LF using AYUSH intervention integrated with allopathy. This is an ayurvedic doctor lead, allopathic nurse assisted model for community dermatology meeting needs of chronic care, acute care and management of infections. The skin care was provided by ayurvedic medicines. Treatment of infections and bacterial entry points were by allopathy and acute episodes of filarial fever that sometimes require hospitalized care was managed by allopathy. Movement essential for prevention of lymph stagnation was achieved by yoga exercises. Compression bandaging of allopathy gave additional lymph drainage to swollen legs.
