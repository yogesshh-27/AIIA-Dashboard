Brief Summary  

This study is a randomized, double blind, parallel group, multi-centre placebo controlled clinical study of Ayurvedic coded drug â€˜Ayush Aâ€™ in the management of mild bronchial asthma (Tamaka Shwasa) that will be conducted in three center in India. The primary outcome measures will be change in asthma controlled questionnaire (ACQ) from the baseline and at the end of 12th week, PEFR assessments of personal best peak flow every 14th days to the end of the trial. The secondary outcome measures will be the change in St. Georgeâ€™s respiratory questionnaire (SGRQ-C) from the baseline and at the end of  12th week.

            Bronchial Asthma is an inflammatory disorder of the airways characterized by increased responsiveness of the trachea and bronchi to various stimuli resulting in narrowing of the airways due to bronchial spasm and edema in bronchial mucous membrane. These changes are reversible either spontaneously or with medication. It may be acute or paroxysmal in type. Most asthmatic patients are diagnosed by triad episodic symptoms (wheezing, cough and dyspnea). In older people, it is not easy to differentiate whether it is certainly a case of asthma, as these symptoms are also seen in other diseases because of poor respiratory function due to ageing. Bronchial Asthma is increasing day by day with the increase in pollution levels and the stressful lifestyle. The prevalence of asthma in elderly people varies from 6.5 to 10%, however the incidence is dependent on environmental condition, occupational patterns, genetic configuration and dietary pattern of the population besides diagnostic criteria adopted by different investigators. Bronchial Asthma is similar to disease entity â€œTamaka Svasaâ€ described in ayurveda. Tamaka svasa is a condition in which difficulty in breathing occurred due to the obstruction to pranavayu due to vitiated kapha. According to ayurveda, main causes of tamaka svasa are unwholesome diet (vidahi, guru, ruksa, abhisyandi diet), exposure to allergen (cold air, dust, smoke, etc.), excessive exertion, etc., which are very close to etiology of bronchial asthma.
